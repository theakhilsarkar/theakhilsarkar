## India's Environmental Crisis: A 15-Year Research Study (2011–2026)

**Project Type:** Individual Research Report
**Subject Area:** Environmental Studies, Geography, Science, Economics, Disaster Management and Social Studies
**Research Period:** 1 January 2011 to 31 October 2026
**Minimum Length:** 100 pages
**Submission Format:** Digitally prepared report in PDF format
**Research Method:** Evidence-based research using government reports, scientific studies, newspapers, credible media, photographs, maps, graphs, tables and documented sources.

# 1. Project Description

India has experienced numerous environmental challenges over the last 15 years, including floods, droughts, heatwaves, cyclones, landslides, forest fires, air and water pollution, deforestation, biodiversity loss, declining wildlife habitats, groundwater depletion, waste pollution and the effects of climate change.

These problems can affect human life, agriculture, public health, infrastructure, economic development, forests, rivers, wetlands, marine ecosystems, birds, insects and other forms of wildlife.

Your task is to conduct a comprehensive research study of India's environmental condition from 2011 to 2026.

You must investigate the major environmental disasters and long-term environmental problems that occurred during this period. For each issue, you must examine when and where it occurred, what caused it, whether human activities contributed to it, how many people and animals were affected, what damage occurred, how the government responded, whether its response was effective, and what could happen in the future.

Your research must also investigate whether India's forests, wildlife populations, bird habitats, rivers, wetlands, biodiversity and other natural resources have improved or deteriorated during this period.

You must use verifiable evidence rather than assumptions or unsupported opinions. Every important fact, statistic, date, quotation, photograph, graph and table must have an identifiable source.

**Central research question:**

*How has India's environment changed between 2011 and 2026, what have been the major causes and consequences of environmental degradation and disasters, how effectively have governments responded, and what does the available evidence indicate about India's environmental future?*

# 2. Main Research Objectives

Your report must achieve the following objectives:

1. Identify and document major environmental disasters and environmental problems in India between 2011 and 2026.
2. Investigate their origins, causes, locations, dates, severity and consequences.
3. Examine the relationship between climate change, natural hazards, human activities and environmental degradation.
4. Document reported human deaths, injuries, displacement, livelihood losses and economic damage.
5. Investigate changes in forests, tree cover, wildlife populations, bird populations, wetlands, rivers and other ecosystems.
6. Research air pollution, water contamination, plastic waste, industrial pollution, soil degradation and groundwater depletion.
7. Examine the role of the Central Government, state governments, local authorities, disaster management agencies, courts and environmental regulators.
8. Evaluate the effectiveness of government policies, environmental laws, restoration programmes and disaster response.
9. Compare environmental conditions across different states, regions and years using verified statistics.
10. Identify the communities, occupations and ecosystems most affected by environmental problems.
11. Use historical evidence and scientific research to examine possible environmental scenarios for 2030, 2040 and 2050.
12. Recommend practical solutions for government authorities, industries, communities, educational institutions and citizens.

2. Cite every statistical figure and numerical claim.
3. Cite every graph and table directly below it.
4. Cite every photograph, map and externally sourced illustration.
5. Cite quotations immediately after the quoted text.
6. Provide direct links to the original reports or articles wherever possible.
7. Record publication dates and access dates where appropriate.
8. Distinguish original research from secondary reporting.
9. If two sources disagree, present the discrepancy and explain which figure you use.
10. Never create fictional references, fake URLs, invented page numbers or unverified statistics.
11. Do not cite an entire website when a specific report or article is available.
12. Keep a reference register linking each important claim to its supporting source.

Wikipedia, social-media posts, AI-generated summaries and unattributed websites may help locate leads, but they must not serve as the primary evidence for important claims.

# 10. Research Integrity and Accuracy Rules

These rules are compulsory.

**A. No fabricated data.** Every statistic must be traceable to a real source.

**B. No unsupported causal claims.** A flood, cyclone or forest fire must not automatically be attributed to climate change. Investigate the evidence for each case.

**C. No false wildlife totals.** Distinguish documented animal deaths from estimated population changes and habitat loss.

**D. No misleading forest statistics.** Separate forest cover, tree cover, natural forest quality, plantation cover and habitat condition.

**E. No mixing incompatible datasets.** Explain differences in measurement, definitions, dates and geographical coverage.

**F. No copied reports.** Summarize evidence in your own words and cite the original source. Use quotation marks for exact quotations.

**G. No unsupported predictions.** Future scenarios must be based on scientific projections or clearly stated assumptions.

**H. No one-sided evaluation.** Report environmental successes and failures, and assess government action against measurable evidence.

**I. No untraceable visuals.** Every externally sourced image, chart or map must have a documented source.

**J. No fake completeness.** Explain where official data are unavailable, incomplete or insufficient to establish a national trend.

# Final Instruction to Students

Your responsibility is to investigate what actually happened to India's environment between 2011 and 2026.

Do not simply collect frightening headlines or write general statements about pollution and climate change. Identify documented events, verify their dates, examine their causes, quantify their impacts wherever reliable data exist, investigate what authorities did, and assess the evidence of subsequent improvement or deterioration.

Pay special attention to the relationship between human well-being and the natural world. Investigate not only human deaths and financial losses but also damage to forests, birds, wildlife, rivers, wetlands, marine ecosystems, soil and biodiversity.

Where evidence is strong, present clear conclusions. Where evidence is uncertain, explain the uncertainty. Where data are missing, acknowledge the limitation.

**The final report must enable a reader to understand what happened, why it happened, who and what was affected, what action was taken, what the evidence says about the results, and what India should do next. Every major conclusion must be supported by traceable evidence.**
